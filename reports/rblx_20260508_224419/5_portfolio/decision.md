**Investment Thesis**: The Research Manager's Underweight thesis is affirmed, and the trader's tactical Sell framing is incorporated into a staged execution — but the rating stops at Underweight rather than outright Sell because the underlying business retains genuine fundamental quality that a full exit would discount too severely.

**Why the bear case wins the core argument:**
The most decisive point in the debate was the aggressive analyst's clarification on the bookings cut: bookings captures total Robux purchased across the entire user base, not just new user onboarding. The simultaneous miss on both DAU and bookings per DAU — when a purely new-user-acquisition friction story would have shown DAU slowing while bookings-per-DAU held — is the critical tell. Management's guidance reduction of nearly $1 billion (from +22–26% to +8–12% bookings growth) implicates existing cohort monetization, not merely the onboarding funnel. The neutral analyst's "temporary global friction" rebuttal, while logically coherent, runs headlong into the bear's most damaging procedural point: management had every opportunity on the earnings call to quantify a recovery timeline and declined. As the aggressive analyst correctly noted, communication discipline in the face of an operational disruption is not evidence of a clean temporary story — it is evidence of insufficient data to commit to one, which is itself a risk factor.

**Why valuation does not provide a floor:**
The $596M Q1 FCF figure that bulls anchor to is materially overstated as an economic earnings proxy. Stripping the ~$275M in quarterly SBC produces ~$321M in economically adjusted FCF. At a ~$30B market cap, the stock trades at roughly 23x SBC-adjusted earnings for a business whose bookings growth just decelerated from ~24% guidance midpoint to ~10% — with no quantified recovery roadmap. The conservative analyst correctly identified that the $6.8B deferred revenue balance is a depleting asset in a slowing replenishment environment: the accounting buffer will make GAAP revenue look stable for a few quarters, but the economic signal from bookings is already deteriorating and conservative risk management acts on economic signals, not accounting lag.

**Why the technical structure demands action:**
Price is 22% below the 50-day SMA ($57.58), 11% below the declining VWMA ($50.35), and sits only ~$3 above the $41.75 critical support level. The MACD at its most negative reading in the 30-day lookback (-3.59) with no crossover signal, combined with the 53.4M-share institutional distribution event on May 1st — the heaviest volume day in the dataset — confirms smart money repositioning, not retail panic. The conservative analyst's gap-risk argument is decisive: the May 1st gap from $55.26 to $42.38 bypassed every stop in between. Stop-losses in this name are aspirational limits, not clean executions. This argues for beginning reduction now rather than relying on the stop as a primary backstop.

**Why the neutral analyst's compression argument is acknowledged but overridden:**
The neutral analyst's risk-window compression point — that at $44.78 vs. the planned $48 entry, a 30–35% reduction achieves the same dollar-risk reduction as 40–50% — is mathematically valid but analytically misapplied. As the conservative analyst correctly rebutted: the window compressed because the thesis deteriorated, not because the risk disappeared. Recalibrating the reduction percentage downward because the stock moved against the plan inverts the logic of risk management. The thesis impairment — not the entry-to-stop arithmetic — must drive position sizing. The neutral analyst's lower reduction target also ignores the asymmetry of gap behavior in this specific stock: a gap-up recovery will be captured with either a 50–60% or 65–70% retained position, while a gap-down through $41.75 scales execution losses directly with retained size.

**Why Underweight rather than Sell:**
The conservative analyst's staged execution framework and the aggressive analyst's total reduction target converge on the right answer. But a full Sell overweights the downside scenario. rblx carries $3.2B in cash against $1.8B in debt, 79% gross margins reflecting genuine platform economics, and no credible competitor in youth gaming-social. The India managing director appointment is correctly discounted as a near-term catalyst given the global age verification rollout, but the long-term TAM optionality has real value. The Research Manager's framing is precise: this is a quality business at the wrong entry point, not a broken business requiring full exit.
